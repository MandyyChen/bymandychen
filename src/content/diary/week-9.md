## week 9: wear the screen

### symposium

i presented my symposium, *wear the screen*, on fashion hardware and wearable displays.

for the demo, i turned lilygo t-display boards (an esp32 with a tiny color screen built in) into hair clips, each powered by a small 3.7v lipo battery. each clip joins wi-fi and shows its ip address, and a web page i built called **say it!** lets you type what you want to say and send it to each clip.

your status doesn't have to look like a status.

### reading: two decades of interactive art (simon penny)

penny looks back at roughly 1985 to 2005 of interactive art and makes a frustrating point: the technology changed constantly, but our understanding of the aesthetics of interaction barely moved. a lot of what artists pioneered with gesture and motion sensing later became consumer products (the wii, the kinect, smartphones) and lost its meaning along the way.

his main shift is from thinking about artworks as objects or representations to thinking about them as performative: meaning comes from doing, through the body, over time. he also separates two kinds of interfaces:

- tools should be transparent and predictable
- aesthetic interaction should sit somewhere between predictable and random, so there's room for surprise

this connects to my symposium topic, fashion hardware. a screen on a hair clip is technically just a tiny display, but once it's on a body it becomes performative: it's worn, it moves when you move, and other people see it before you do. the hardware is the same, the meaning comes from the wearing.
