today, we went to ceid, did makerspace onboarding, training and closed with a interesting exercise. we listed products we wished existed, i immediately thought about problems i face day to day and things that would resolve it (without considering restrictions of physics). we got 10 minutes to translate one of these products into prototypes using materials at the ceid. so, we created a robot that could find your missing things for you:

1) his name was found-o, speak to him and he will find your left airpod, missing sock, or keys.
2) we gave him the minimal functionalities: wheels, signals, and arms.
3) rapid prototyping gets messy, but scrappiness is the best way to start.
4) interfaces get tricky, especially creating new ones that don't yet exist.
5) if you're thinking big, think bigger.

i like the concept of norman doors. perhaps if we make enough norman doors, it eliminates all bad designs until we find the right one.
or it'll lay the foundations of what NOT to do. i'll continuing messing around with this idea.
