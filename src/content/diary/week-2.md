this week, we learned how to solder at the ceid and how to tell a good joint from a bad one.
i hadn't soldered in almost 5 years so it was a great refresh. we received our xiao esp32-c6 and
soldered on the header pins as the takehome assignment. i definitely did not do a great job and
used so much solder that it looks like cake frosting, but none of the joints are bridging so it
should still function correctly. after that i downloaded the arduino ide and installed the esp32
board package so i could plug the xiao in over usb-c and get the ide talking to it.

i'm definitely excited to start breadboarding, since for homework we went through the labs on
components, breadboards, and electronics. a lot of components like the servo motor and dc motor
are incredibly familiar to me (thank you medtronic) but i hope to test out more of the sensors and
relearn how to cut and strip wire.

### reading: a brief rant on the future of interaction design (bret victor)

victor wrote this after watching a "future of productivity" vision video, and his point is simple: the future it shows is just pictures under glass. every interaction is a finger sliding across a flat screen, which ignores almost everything our hands can do.

he walks through how much our hands actually sense:

- weight
- texture
- pressure and resistance
- the way you know how full a glass is just by holding it

then he asks why our most advanced tools give us less feedback than a book or a jar.

this one landed for me as someone who spends most of her design time in figma. so much of my work lives behind glass and i rarely question it. soldering this week was a funny contrast, because you can feel and see whether a joint is good (or, in my case, cake frosting).

the line i keep coming back to: "a tool addresses human needs by amplifying human capabilities." that sentence kind of feels like the thesis of this class.
